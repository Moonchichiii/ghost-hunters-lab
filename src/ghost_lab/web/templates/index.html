01 / The hunt — A real experiment. A strange question.

I went looking for a ghost inside an AI model.

I found a ghost-related signal inside Gemma. I altered its internal activity, tested the effects against five random directions, and measured what else changed.

02 / The myth

He challenged the myth.
I borrowed the metaphor.

“the dogma of the Ghost in the Machine”

Gilbert Ryle, The Concept of Mind (1949)

Ryle challenged a way of thinking about minds. I'm not testing whether AI is conscious or possessed. “Ghosts and spirits” is a published label for a feature identified using Gemma Scope 2. I wanted to see what happens when I perturb that direction.

03 / The method

Find it. Change it. Check the consequences.

  1. 01

    Find

    I extracted the decoder direction for SAE feature #23871 from a published Gemma Scope 2 checkpoint.

  2. 02

    Change

    I injected that direction at layer 22 while Gemma generated text. The original model weights remained unchanged.

  3. 03

    Compare

    I used 20 previously untested prompts, five norm-matched random directions, and recorded the next-token scores.

05 / The consequences

The ghost wasn't the only thing that moved.

I tested 12 unrelated next-token predictions: geography, colors, language and everyday knowledge. The intervention affected these probabilities too.

These are exact-token side-effect probes, not a general capability or accuracy benchmark. A changed probability does not mean the model has lost an ability.

06 / The verdict

I moved a signal.
I didn't erase a ghost.

The experiments demonstrate measurable effects from activation steering in an open-weight model. They do not establish selective control of an entire concept, preservation of general capabilities, or a permanent change to the model's weights.

Weight-level exorcism: not attempted. Every figure on this page comes from the recorded experiment summary.
